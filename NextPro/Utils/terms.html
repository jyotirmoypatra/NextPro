3. Definitions

Access Event: any instance in which a credential, biometric scan, or mobile device is presented to or read by ZYLX hardware, whether access is granted or denied.

Biometric Data: data resulting from specific technical processing relating to the physical, physiological, or behavioral characteristics of a natural person which allow or confirm the unique identification of that person, including facial geometry, faceprints, facial templates, and similar derived data generated from camera images for the purpose of facial recognition or face matching.

Biometric Identifier: a retina or iris scan, fingerprint, voiceprint, or scan of hand or face geometry, or a faceprint or facial template derived therefrom, as that term or its equivalent is defined under applicable biometric privacy statutes.

Credential: any means used to request or obtain access through the Services, including an RFID or proximity card, key fob, PIN code, mobile credential (Bluetooth, NFC, or QR-code-based), or biometric enrollment.

End User: an individual who is issued, or who enrolls, a Credential to obtain physical access to a premises secured by the Services, including employees, members, residents, contractors, and visitors of an Organization Administrator.

Facial Recognition Data: Biometric Data specifically derived from an image or video of a person's face for the purpose of identifying or verifying that person's identity, including any mathematical representation, template, embedding, or vector generated from such image.

Organization Administrator: the business, property owner, employer, landlord, or other entity that has contracted with ZYLX (directly or through a Reseller) to deploy the Services at one or more premises, and that configures access permissions, enrolls or authorizes the enrollment of End Users, and otherwise administers the Services for its own organizational purposes.

Personal Data: any information relating to an identified or identifiable natural person, consistent with the meaning given to that term (or the term “personal information”) under applicable data protection law.

Reseller: a third party authorized by ZYLX to sell, distribute, install, configure, integrate, or provide managed or support services for the Services, whether on a one-time or ongoing basis.

Services: the ZYLX access control platform described in Section 2, including all related hardware, software, mobile applications, web portals, and APIs.

Special Category Data / Sensitive Personal Data: Personal Data that receives heightened protection under applicable law, including Biometric Data, and, depending on jurisdiction, data revealing racial or ethnic origin, health information, or other categories specified by local law.

Video Data: any still image, video recording, or live video stream captured by a camera installed at or near a ZYLX access point.

5. Special Provisions for Biometric Data and Facial Recognition

Because facial recognition involves Sensitive Personal Data in virtually every jurisdiction that regulates it, this section sets out additional, specific commitments that apply whenever facial recognition functionality is enabled at a premises.

5.1 Enrollment is opt-in and requires affirmative consent

Facial recognition will not be activated for any individual End User unless: (a) the Organization Administrator has enabled the feature for the relevant premises; and (b) the individual End User has provided affirmative, informed, written or electronic consent specific to the collection, storage, and use of their facial biometric data, separate from general acceptance of this Policy where required by applicable law (including Illinois BIPA, Texas CUBI, Washington's biometric law, and EU/UK law treating biometric data as a special category requiring explicit consent under GDPR Article 9).

Where an Organization Administrator is legally permitted to rely on an alternative legal basis (for example, in an employment context where local law permits processing necessary for entering into or performing an employment-related obligation, subject to strict necessity and proportionality requirements), the Organization Administrator is responsible for documenting that basis and for any required works council, union, or employee-representative consultation, and for providing legally adequate notice to affected individuals before enabling the feature.

5.2 You always have a non-biometric alternative

ZYLX requires every Organization Administrator that enables facial recognition to also make available a reasonable, non-biometric alternative method of access (such as an RFID card, mobile credential, or PIN) for any End User who declines to enroll in or withdraws from facial recognition, and to ensure that declining or withdrawing does not result in denial of service, employment, or access to facilities to which the individual is otherwise entitled, except where a specific facility has a documented, narrowly tailored security justification disclosed to the individual in advance. If you believe you have been denied a non-biometric alternative in violation of this commitment, contact us immediately at the email address in Section 1.

5.3 What happens to facial images and templates

  • At enrollment, one or more facial images are captured and used to generate a mathematical template (a numerical representation of facial geometry). Depending on system configuration, the raw enrollment image may be retained for re-enrollment and audit purposes or discarded after template generation.

  • At each access attempt, a live image is captured, converted into a template, and compared against stored enrollment templates to determine a match. The live comparison image is, by default, not retained beyond the time necessary to complete the match determination, unless the Organization Administrator has configured extended retention for security audit purposes, in which case Section 11 (Retention) applies.

  • Facial templates are stored in encrypted form, both in transit and at rest, and are logically segregated from general video footage.

  • We do not sell, lease, trade, or disclose Biometric Data to any third party for that third party's own independent use. We do not disclose Biometric Data to third parties except as described in Section 9 (subprocessors and service providers acting on our instructions, legal compliance, or with your separate explicit consent).

  • We do not use facial recognition templates to create marketing profiles, to infer emotion, sentiment, mood, health, or demographic attributes, or for any purpose unrelated to access control and security, except where an Organization Administrator has enabled a specific, disclosed analytics feature and obtained any additionally required consent.

5.4 Retention and destruction of biometric data

Biometric Data is retained only for as long as necessary to fulfill the purpose for which it was collected, and in any event is permanently deleted on the earliest of: (a) the date the initial purpose for collection has been satisfied (for example, the End User's relationship with the Organization Administrator ends, the End User withdraws consent, or the credential is revoked); (b) three years from the End User's last interaction with the Services, unless a shorter period is required by applicable law (Illinois BIPA, for example, requires deletion within three years of the individual's last interaction, and Texas and Washington law impose similar or shorter limits which we follow where applicable); or (c) a shorter period specified by the Organization Administrator's configuration or by applicable law in the End User's jurisdiction. Section 11 sets out our general retention schedule and Section 19.4 sets out how End Users can request earlier deletion.

5.5 No public or third-party biometric databases

We do not contribute facial templates to, or match against, any publicly available facial recognition database, watchlist, or third-party identification service. Matching is performed solely against the enrollment templates of individuals specifically enrolled by the relevant Organization Administrator for that Organization Administrator's own premises.

7. How We Use Personal Data

We use Personal Data collected through the Services for the following purposes:

  • to operate physical access control, including granting, denying, logging, and auditing Access Events;

  • to enroll, verify, and authenticate End Users via RFID, PIN, mobile credential, or facial recognition where enabled;

  • to provide security monitoring through integrated cameras, including live viewing and recorded footage review by authorized personnel of the Organization Administrator;

  • to investigate security incidents, policy violations, unauthorized access attempts, tailgating, or safety events;

  • to maintain, troubleshoot, and improve the reliability, performance, and security of the Services, including diagnosing hardware and software issues;

  • to communicate with you about your account, service updates, planned maintenance, or security alerts;

  • to provide customer and technical support to Organization Administrators, Resellers, and, where applicable, End Users;

  • to process billing and payments for Organization Administrators and Resellers;

  • to detect, investigate, and prevent fraud, abuse, or violations of our terms;

  • to comply with applicable law, respond to lawful requests from public authorities, and establish, exercise, or defend legal claims;

  • to generate aggregated, de-identified statistics regarding system usage and occupancy for the benefit of Organization Administrators (such aggregation never includes Biometric Data in identifiable form); and

  • with appropriate additional notice and consent where required, to develop and improve underlying access-control and computer-vision algorithms, using data that has been de-identified or aggregated wherever technically feasible, and using identifiable Biometric Data for model training or improvement only where an Organization Administrator and affected End Users have provided specific, separate consent to that secondary use.

We do not use facial recognition or other biometric data to make decisions producing legal effects or similarly significant effects concerning an individual based solely on automated processing without human involvement, except for the automated grant or denial of a physical access request itself, which is the core, disclosed function of the Services. Access denial based on a failed match always permits a fallback verification process (such as manual verification by on-site personnel or use of an alternative credential), where the Organization Administrator has configured one, and we encourage all Organization Administrators to provide such a fallback.

Video Data is used solely for the security, access verification, incident investigation, and service-operation purposes described in this Policy. Cameras and biometric capture devices must not be installed by Organization Administrators in restrooms, locker rooms, breastfeeding/lactation rooms, prayer or religious observance rooms, or other locations where individuals have a heightened reasonable expectation of privacy. Implementation notes for development

  • Both checkboxes must be unchecked by default and presented to every user in the same flow; acceptance requires an affirmative, distinct user action for each (no pre-checked boxes, no bundled “accept all” toggle that substitutes for the biometric-specific checkbox).

  • The Policy text (or a clearly labeled link to the full Policy that the user can reasonably be expected to access before checking the box) must be presented above or alongside the checkboxes at the time of acceptance.

  • Record, for each checkbox independently: the user ID, the exact Policy version/Effective Date accepted, a timestamp, and (where feasible) IP address, in an auditable log. This record should be retrievable to demonstrate consent if ever challenged.

  • At the moment a given premises actually enables facial recognition, trigger the enrollment-time confirmation described above for every affected user before capturing their first enrollment image, even if that user already checked Checkbox 2 previously.

  • If the Policy is later updated in a way that is material — particularly any change to biometric data practices — re-present both checkboxes and require renewed acceptance before continuing; do not carry forward a prior acceptance of an older version as acceptance of the new one.

  • Provide an accessible, in-account mechanism for a user to later withdraw Checkbox 2 / enrollment-time consent (i.e., turn off facial recognition and revert to a non-biometric credential) without needing to contact support, consistent with Section 5.2 and Section 17.4, and log that withdrawal with the same rigor as the original consent.

  • This implementation pattern applies uniformly to every user of the Services — whoever is creating an account or enrolling a credential checks the same two boxes, regardless of whether they are doing so as an individual end user, as an administrator setting up their own access, or as a reseller-managed account; the substantive obligations that differ by role are addressed in Sections 19–21 of the Policy itself, not in the acceptance mechanism.
